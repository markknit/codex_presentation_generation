## Corrections, deviations and decisions

- Source L5 proposes human saliva, including a pooled-saliva option. The deck flags this for school and teacher biosafety review and does not endorse pooling or invent an alternative. The source-permitted water and boiled-saliva controls are distinct comparisons; the teacher must choose and record one and its limits.
- Corrected overgeneralised claims: salivary amylase produces smaller products including maltose, and other enzymes including maltase produce glucose; amylase is a protein but not all enzymes are proteins. Cold can slow activity without proving zero activity at 0 °C; 37 °C is not a universal optimum. No expected colours are presented as observed results.

- The source calls the Wanjiru case real; its independent provenance and nutrient estimates were not supplied. It is labelled source case data, not a diagnosis. The case graph does not by itself prove which food or hormone caused the pattern. No pupils are asked to fast, test blood or disclose health information.
- Source resource links on the hidden glance slide are provided only as unverified links; preview content for accuracy and spoilers, and verify ARES offline access. Additional source URLs are listed below.
- The original final paper/key assumes exact observations, overstates enzyme temperature rules and treats some inferred physiological details as measured facts. The adapted student assessment requests actual observations; the teacher must review and adapt the source key and rubric.
- Whole-unit outcomes mention vitamin C tests and investigation of enzymes in living tissues, with catalase in values, but the six-lesson activities do not clearly provide separate vitamin C or catalase investigations. Source-owner decision is open; no new practical was invented.
- All graphics, line plots, tables and labels are editable except the contextual illustrations and institutional logos. Source files remain unchanged. Native PowerPoint/Presenter View and teacher approval remain open.
